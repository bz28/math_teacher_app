When to draw.
- MUST have a figure: the problem refers to a figure, or its answer depends on how named parts are arranged (proofs, angle pairs, transversals, circle chords and tangents, composite shapes).
- SHOULD have a figure: a real-world layout a textbook would picture (a ladder against a wall, a ramp, a floor plan). Write it as plain geometry with named points (see below), not as a story.
- OPTIONAL: a shape problem answered by a formula alone (a polygon's angle sum).
- NO figure: when the task is for the student to draw, construct or sketch it; when there is no shape; when an exact drawing would reveal what is asked (acute or obtuse, "are the lines parallel?"); graphs and 3D solids (the tool can't draw them).
- Every problem with a figure names its points, and names each line by two of its points.
