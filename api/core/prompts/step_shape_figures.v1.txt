Per-step geometry figures:
- For geometry problems where the construction evolves step by step (drop a perpendicular, draw an altitude, construct a chord, mark an inscribed angle), attach a `figure_spec` to the step that introduces the new construction. The renderer draws the updated figure with the new mark, replacing the previous step's figure.
- For purely algebraic steps inside a geometry solution, OMIT the figure_spec — repeating the same diagram with no visual change is noise.
- Two supported shapes: triangle and circle (set `shape` accordingly). Other shape categories (polygons, coordinate plane, transformations) aren't supported yet — describe in prose rather than emit a wrong shape.
- Triangles: single-character vertex names; for 90° angles use `right_angle_at` (also draws the square marker).
- Circles: positive `radius` + named points on the circumference with angle in degrees CCW from the positive x-axis. Chords are two-character identifiers.
- Points OUTSIDE the circle (tangent–secant, two secants / power of a point, two tangents): use `external_points` — each is the intersection of two lines (a `tangent_at` an on-circle point, or a `secant_through` two on-circle points). The renderer places the point and draws the lines; don't fake an external point with a chord between circumference points. For two secants from one external point, put the two points nearest the external point close together on the arc facing it (e.g. around 30° and 330°) and the two far points on the other side (e.g. around 95° and 265°), so both secants converge to the external point off one side; never make the two chords parallel (parallel lines never meet).
- Polygons (4+ sides — squares, hexagons, etc.): use `shape='polygon'` with `n_sides` (regular) or `vertex_positions` (irregular). Don't use polygon mode for triangles — `shape='triangle'` has the constraint solver.
- Compound (circle inscribed in / circumscribed around a triangle): use `shape='triangle'` and set the optional `inscribed_circle` or `circumscribed_circle` field. The renderer computes the circle from the triangle's own geometry — don't try to coordinate two separate shapes.
- Reuse the names from the problem text so students can track which point is which across steps.
