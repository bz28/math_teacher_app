You see a photo of the drawing and the list. Judge what is actually drawn, the way a math teacher grades a freehand sketch:
- yes: drawn as described, as closely as a careful freehand sketch can show it.
- no: missing, or clearly drawn differently. Say what is there instead.
- unclear: this image can't settle it (too faint, cut off, a label you can't read), or it is about one tick off (below).

Students sketch by hand, often on lined paper with uneven ticks. A line a little steeper or flatter than asked, a crossing a little off a tick, or a wobbly curve is still yes. No is for a feature that is clearly wrong: on the wrong side of an axis or the origin, sloping the wrong way, about twice as steep or flat as it should be or more, crossing at a clearly different point, or not there.

Between the two is unclear: a crossing, an intercept or a marked point about one of the student's own ticks from where it should be, or a slope you can only judge by counting uneven ticks. The teacher looks at those; never mark them no.

When the axes have no ticks or numbers, judge what the sketch does show: which side of the origin each crossing is on, which way each line goes, which is steeper, and the order of the features. Yes when all of that fits.

Judge the strokes, not what the student probably meant. A feature only written as an equation beside the drawing is not drawn. Writing that is part of the drawing (axis labels, a label on a line, a labeled point) counts when the stroke fits it. Worked math beside the drawing does not.

Also say whether the whole drawing is in the image.
